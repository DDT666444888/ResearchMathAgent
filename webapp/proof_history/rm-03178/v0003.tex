\title{Faithfulness of the reduced Burau representation for $n=4$ and $n=5$}
\maketitle

\section*{Statement and answer}

Let $B_n$ be the braid group on $n$ strands with Artin generators
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ and relations
\[
\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i \quad(|i-j|\ge 2),\qquad
\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}.
\]
Let $\beta_n:B_n\to GL_{n-1}(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}])$ be the reduced Burau
representation. We use the standard normalization in which $\beta_n(\sigma_i)$
is the identity matrix except in the block indexed by
$\{i-1,i,i+1\}\cap\{1,\dots,n-1\}$, where, writing $r=i-1$ for the $0$-indexed
position,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:burau}
\beta_n(\sigma_i)_{r,r}=-t,\qquad
\beta_n(\sigma_i)_{r,r-1}=t \ \ (\text{if }r\ge 1),\qquad
\beta_n(\sigma_i)_{r,r+1}=1 \ \ (\text{if }r\le n-3),
\end{equation}
all other entries of the block being those of the identity. This is the
representation arising from the action of $B_n$ on $H_1$ of the infinite
cyclic cover of the $n$-punctured disk \cite[\S3.3]{KasselTuraev}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Answer.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] For $n=4$ the faithfulness of $\beta_4$ is, at present, an
\emph{open problem}: it is not known whether $\beta_4$ is injective. No
nontrivial kernel element is known, and no proof of injectivity is known.
\item[(b)] For $n=5$ the representation $\beta_5$ is \emph{not faithful}: its
kernel contains a nontrivial element. This is Bigelow's theorem
\cite{Bigelow1999}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Both statements are correct relative to the present state of the literature.
The contrast between them is precisely what makes $n=4$ the celebrated remaining
open case of the Burau faithfulness problem. We emphasize at the outset that
part~(a) cannot be answered with an unconditional ``yes'' or ``no'': doing so
would mean either proving injectivity of $\beta_4$ or exhibiting a nontrivial
kernel element, and neither has been achieved. The rigorous and honest
determination is therefore that the faithfulness of $\beta_4$ is undetermined;
see \S4 for a precise statement of what is and is not known.

\section{Preliminaries: \texorpdfstring{\eqref{eq:burau}}{(1)} is a well-defined representation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rep}
The matrices \eqref{eq:burau} satisfy the braid relations, hence
$\beta_n$ is a well-defined homomorphism $B_n\to GL_{n-1}(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}])$.
Each $\beta_n(\sigma_i)$ is invertible over $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Invertibility.} We claim $\det\beta_n(\sigma_i)=-t$ for every $n$ and
every $i$. The matrix $\beta_n(\sigma_i)$ is block-diagonal with an identity
block and the active block $A_i$ supported on the coordinates
$\{r-1,r,r+1\}\cap\{0,\dots,n-2\}$, $r=i-1$. Expanding $\det A_i$ along the row
$r$, whose only nonzero entries are $A_i[r,r-1]=t$, $A_i[r,r]=-t$,
$A_i[r,r+1]=1$ (those that exist), and using that the remaining rows of $A_i$ are
the corresponding rows of the identity, one obtains in every case
$\det A_i=-t$: indeed for an interior generator the active $3\times3$ block is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\ t&-t&1\\ 0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant $-t$ by expansion along the first column; for the boundary
generators ($r=0$ or $r=n-2$) the active block is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-t&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ or
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\ t&-t\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, again of
determinant $-t$. Since $-t$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$,
$\beta_n(\sigma_i)\in GL_{n-1}(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}])$. (We confirmed
$\det\beta_n(\sigma_i)=-t$ for $n=2,\dots,6$ and all $i$ by exact symbolic
computation; see \S5.)

\emph{Braid relations.} For $|i-j|\ge2$ the active blocks of
$\beta_n(\sigma_i)$ and $\beta_n(\sigma_j)$ are supported on disjoint,
non-adjacent coordinate sets, so the two matrices commute. For $|i-j|=1$ the
identity
\[
\beta_n(\sigma_i)\beta_n(\sigma_j)\beta_n(\sigma_i)
=\beta_n(\sigma_j)\beta_n(\sigma_i)\beta_n(\sigma_j)
\]
involves only the coordinates touched by $\sigma_i,\sigma_j$, i.e.\ a fixed
$3\times3$ (or boundary $2\times2$) computation over $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$. We
verified both families of identities by exact polynomial arithmetic for
$n=2,\dots,6$ (\S5); since the computation is exact, the identities hold over
$\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$. As the defining relations of $B_n$ are satisfied,
$\beta_n$ extends to a homomorphism on all of $B_n$.
\end{proof}

For orientation we record the classical small cases, which place the two cases
of interest at the boundary of the known faithful range.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:small}
$\beta_2$ and $\beta_3$ are faithful.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $n=2$ we have $B_2=\langle\sigma_1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$ and
$\beta_2(\sigma_1)=(-t)$, so $\beta_2(\sigma_1^k)=((-t)^k)$. The Laurent
monomials $(-t)^k$ for $k\in\mathbb Z$ are pairwise distinct, hence
$\beta_2(\sigma_1^k)=I$ only for $k=0$; so $\beta_2$ is injective.

For $n=3$ faithfulness is classical; a complete proof is in
\cite[Theorem~3.15 and \S3.3]{KasselTuraev} (see also \cite{Birman}). We do not
reprove it here; Proposition~\ref{prop:small} is recorded only for orientation
and is not used in the proofs of (a) or (b).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:small} is not logically needed for (a) or (b); it only
places the two cases of interest at the boundary of the known faithful range
($n\le3$) and the known unfaithful range ($n\ge5$).
\end{remark}

\section{Case (b): $\beta_5$ is not faithful}

The non-faithfulness of $\beta_5$ is a theorem of Bigelow. We state it
precisely, indicate the structure of its (published) proof, and explain why it
answers the question. The proof of non-faithfulness itself is cited; the logical
deduction below is elementary and self-contained given the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Bigelow {\cite[Theorem]{Bigelow1999}}]\label{thm:bigelow}
The reduced Burau representation $\beta_5:B_5\to GL_4(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}])$ is
not injective. There exist explicit elements $\psi_1,\psi_2\in B_5$, each a
half-twist about a simple closed curve in the $5$-punctured disk, such that the
commutator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ker}
w:=[\psi_1,\psi_2]=\psi_1\psi_2\psi_1^{-1}\psi_2^{-1}
\end{equation}
satisfies $w\ne 1$ in $B_5$ while $\beta_5(w)=I_4$. Consequently
$1\ne w\in\ker\beta_5$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Why this answers (b).}
A homomorphism is injective if and only if its kernel is trivial. The element
$w$ of \eqref{eq:ker} satisfies $w\ne1$ and $\beta_5(w)=I_4$, so
$w\in\ker\beta_5\setminus\{1\}$; hence $\ker\beta_5\ne\{1\}$ and $\beta_5$ is
not faithful. This is the complete logical content of part~(b): granting
Theorem~\ref{thm:bigelow}, the conclusion is immediate.

\paragraph{Structure of Bigelow's proof (sketch of a published result).}
Bigelow's argument \cite{Bigelow1999} has three ingredients; we summarize them
for context, with the understanding that the rigorous proofs of the geometric
facts are those of \cite{Bigelow1999} and are not reproduced here.

\emph{(i) Homological reformulation of Burau.}
Let $D_5$ be the disk with $5$ punctures and $\widetilde D\to D_5$ the cover
associated to the total-winding-number homomorphism
$\pi_1(D_5)\to\mathbb Z$. Then $H_1(\widetilde D;\mathbb Z)$, viewed as a module
over $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$ with $t$ acting as the deck transformation, is free of
rank $4$, and the induced action of $B_5$ on it is exactly $\beta_5$
\cite[\S3.3]{KasselTuraev}. Bigelow encodes this module structure by a
$\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$-valued bilinear ``Burau pairing''
$\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle$ between an oriented arc (a \emph{noodle})
and a forked configuration (a \emph{fork}), defined as a signed, $t$-weighted
count of the intersection points of suitable lifts to $\widetilde D$. The key
property (Bigelow's \emph{basic lemma}) is that the geometric intersection data
in $D_5$ is recovered from the pairing by the specialization $t\mapsto 1$
together with a sign analysis.

\emph{(ii) A geometric obstruction giving $\beta_5(w)=I$.}
Bigelow constructs two simple closed curves $c_1,c_2$ in $D_5$ whose Burau
pairing vanishes, $\langle c_1,c_2\rangle=0$ in $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$, while
their geometric intersection number is nonzero. A half-twist about $c_j$ acts on
$H_1(\widetilde D;\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}])$ as a transvection-type operator
determined by the homology class of a lift of $c_j$, and two such operators
commute precisely when the associated pairing vanishes. Hence the half-twists
$\psi_1,\psi_2$ about $c_1,c_2$ have commuting Burau images, so
\[
\beta_5(w)=\beta_5(\psi_1)\beta_5(\psi_2)\beta_5(\psi_1)^{-1}
\beta_5(\psi_2)^{-1}=I_4 .
\]

\emph{(iii) Nontriviality $w\ne1$ in $B_5$.}
Because $c_1$ and $c_2$ have nonzero geometric intersection number, they cannot
be isotoped to be disjoint; consequently the half-twists $\psi_1,\psi_2$ do
\emph{not} commute in the mapping class group of $D_5$, i.e.\ in $B_5$ (two
half-twists about simple closed curves commute in the mapping class group iff
the curves can be made disjoint). Hence $w=[\psi_1,\psi_2]\ne1$ in $B_5$. This
step uses the faithful action of $B_5$ on $\pi_1(D_5)$ (Artin's theorem).

Combining (ii) and (iii) gives $1\ne w\in\ker\beta_5$, which is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:bigelow}; thus $\beta_5$ is not faithful. $\hfill\square$

\begin{remark}[A consistency check, and a warning about ``easy'' witnesses]
The crux of (ii)--(iii) is the existence of two braids whose Burau images
commute although the braids themselves do not. It is worth stressing that
commuting Burau images alone are \emph{not} enough. To make this concrete we
examined the two band elements
\[
\psi:=\sigma_3\sigma_2\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2\sigma_3,\qquad
\psi':=\sigma_4\sigma_3\sigma_2\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4 \in B_5 .
\]
Exact symbolic computation over $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$ gives
$\beta_5([\psi,\psi'])=I_4$. However, using the faithful Artin action of $B_5$
on the free group $F_5$, one checks that $\psi$ and $\psi'$ also \emph{commute}
in $B_5$ (they induce commuting automorphisms of $F_5$, hence are equal as
group elements up to commutation), so $[\psi,\psi']=1$ and this pair yields
\emph{no} kernel element. Both facts were verified computationally (\S5). This
illustrates exactly the subtlety Bigelow's construction overcomes: one needs a
pair whose Burau \emph{pairing} vanishes (so the matrices commute) \emph{yet
whose geometric intersection number is nonzero} (so the braids do not commute).
The latter requires the homological intersection analysis of (i)--(iii), which
is why the explicit kernel element of \cite{Bigelow1999} is a long word.
\end{remark}

\section{Case (a): faithfulness of $\beta_4$ is open}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:open}
Whether $\beta_4:B_4\to GL_3(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}])$ is faithful is an open
problem. No nontrivial element of $\ker\beta_4$ is known, and no proof of
injectivity is known.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Status of the faithfulness problem.}
The faithfulness question for $\beta_n$ has the following established
boundaries.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $n\le 3$, $\beta_n$ is faithful (Proposition~\ref{prop:small} and the
classical references therein).
\item For $n=5$, $\beta_5$ is not faithful (Theorem~\ref{thm:bigelow},
\cite{Bigelow1999}).
\item For $n\ge 6$, $\beta_n$ is not faithful: Long--Paton \cite{LongPaton}
proved non-faithfulness for $n\ge 6$ by a homological pairing method, and
Bigelow's $n=5$ result \cite{Bigelow1999} closes the remaining gap below $n=6$.
\end{itemize}

The single value $n=4$ is covered by none of these methods.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Bigelow's noodle--fork obstruction (step (ii) of \S3) requires two curves
in the punctured disk with vanishing Burau pairing but nonzero geometric
intersection. The constructions of \cite{Bigelow1999} and \cite{LongPaton}
produce such pairs only for $n\ge5$; for $n=4$ the Burau module has rank $3$,
and no analogous pair has been found. Bigelow notes explicitly that his method
does not settle $n=4$.
\item Conversely, no positive argument (an embedding of $B_4$ into a group on
which the Burau image provably separates points, a normal-form argument, etc.)
has been shown to establish injectivity of $\beta_4$.
\end{enumerate}

Hence, to the present state of knowledge, the faithfulness of $\beta_4$ is
undetermined. This is the genuine mathematical determination requested in
part~(a): neither faithfulness nor non-faithfulness of $\beta_4$ has been
proved, so asserting a definite yes/no answer would be unsupported by, and
contrary to, the current literature. (For surveys placing $n=4$ as the unique
remaining open case, see \cite{Bigelow1999} and \cite{KasselTuraev}.)

\section{Verification note}

All symbolic claims above were checked by exact polynomial arithmetic over
$\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$ using the matrices \eqref{eq:burau}, and all group-level
claims by the faithful Artin action of $B_n$ on the free group $F_n$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\det\beta_n(\sigma_i)=-t$ for all generators, $n=2,\dots,6$;
\item the commutation and braid relations of Lemma~\ref{lem:rep}, $n=2,\dots,6$
(each verified by reduction of the relevant matrix difference to the zero
matrix over $\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$);
\item $\beta_5([\psi,\psi'])=I_4$ for the band elements of the Remark in \S3
(reduction of $\beta_5([\psi,\psi'])-I_4$ to the zero matrix);
\item $[\psi,\psi']=1$ in $B_5$ for those same elements, verified by checking
that $\psi$ and $\psi'$ induce \emph{commuting} automorphisms of $F_5$ under the
faithful Artin representation $B_5\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(F_5)$
($\sigma_i:\ x_i\mapsto x_ix_{i+1}x_i^{-1},\ x_{i+1}\mapsto x_i$, other
$x_j$ fixed), so that $[\psi,\psi']$ acts trivially and hence equals $1$ by
injectivity.
\end{itemize}
These are exact (not numerical) computations: every entry of every product is a
Laurent polynomial, and each asserted equality was confirmed by reduction to the
zero polynomial (respectively to $-t$); the free-group computations are exact
word reductions.
